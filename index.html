Bio

I’m completing my PhD in Applied Mathematics at Université Paris-Saclay, within Centre Borelli, in collaboration with DXOMARK. I’m co-advised by Gabriele Facciolo and Jean-Michel Morel.

I develop interpretable methods for image quality assessment and computational photography. My research connects camera pipelines, portrait rendering, and vision-language models—making visual quality easier to measure, understand, and improve. In November 2026, I will begin a postdoctoral position working with Laurent Oudre and Gabriele Facciolo on the NeuroCoRe project. My research will focus on multimodal learning, video understanding, and time-series modeling to study human behavior and interaction, using data from VR/AR environments and wearable devices such as smart glasses. Prior to research, I received the MVA MS degree from ENS Paris-Saclay. Before pursuing my master’s degree, I worked at Renault in South Korea on the development and validation of vehicle infotainment systems.

I’m always happy to exchange ideas, discuss research, or explore potential collaborations—feel free to get in touch!

WACV 2027 PORTRAIT QUALITY

Interpretable Face Contrast Modeling for Smartphone Portrait Rendering

Yujin Cho, Benoit Pochon, Jean-Michel Morel, Gabriele Facciolo

Decomposing face contrast into overall, eye, and micro-contrast, using a controlled dataset of 508 captures from 29 smartphones to connect interpretable metrics with human judgments.

Code · GitHub ↗
Research details

Decomposing face contrast. We characterize face contrast through four complementary perceptual dimensions: overall contrast, eye contrast, micro-contrast, and balanced contrast. This decomposition makes it possible to analyze distinct aspects of portrait rendering beyond a single global contrast score.

Dedicated metrics aligned with human perception. We propose interpretable metrics tailored to these dimensions and grounded in human perceptual judgments. Realistic mannequin portraits, semantic face masks, and pairwise perceptual annotations support a controlled study of how each dimension contributes to perceived face contrast and balanced contrast preference.

CVPR WORKSHOP · NTIRE 2026 CAMERA PIPELINES

Lightweight Unpaired Smartphone ISP Transfer with Semantic Pseudo-Pairing

Yujin Cho, Flavien Armangeon, Yanhao Li

Learning smartphone color rendering from unpaired images with semantic pseudo-pairing and a compact 7K-parameter network.

Paper · CVF ↗
Research details

The method uses DINOv2 features and Fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport to retrieve semantically related source and target images. It achieved an average rank of third across evaluation metrics on the hidden test set of the NTIRE 2026 Learned Smartphone ISP Challenge.

ELECTRONIC IMAGING · 2026 ✧ BEST GENAI STUDENT PAPER

Adapting DeQA-Score for Attribute-specific Portrait Quality Assessment

Yujin Cho, Minh Khang Tran, Benoit Pochon, Jean-Michel Morel, Gabriele Facciolo, Sira Ferradans

Adapting a vision-language quality model to assess portrait texture and noise separately, using LoRA fine-tuning and pairwise human judgments.

Paper · IS&T ↗
Research details

From overall quality to individual attributes. DeQA-Score is trained to predict global Mean Opinion Scores (MOS), whereas portrait evaluation needs to distinguish texture preservation from noise suppression. We investigate how to adapt this model to attribute-specific Just Objectionable Difference (JOD) scores derived from pairwise human comparisons.

Baseline analysis and adaptation. Experiments on controlled mannequin portraits examine different prompts and spatial input configurations, revealing limited attribute sensitivity in the pretrained model. We then use LoRA fine-tuning on realistic portraits with separate texture and noise annotations to align predictions with attribute-level human judgments.
